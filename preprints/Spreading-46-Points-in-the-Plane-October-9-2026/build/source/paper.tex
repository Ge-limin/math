\begin{abstract}
We place 46 points in the plane so that the squared ratio of the largest to the smallest distance between them is at most 43.71614+. Erich Friedman's table of best known configurations lists n up to 31. Deleting a point from our 47-point configuration gives 44.59453; this one is smaller. The ratio is computed exactly from the published coordinates. We do not claim it is optimal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result}
\begin{theorem}
There are 46 points in the plane with
\[ \Bigl(\frac{\max d}{\min d}\Bigr)^2 = 43.71614\ldots \]
\end{theorem}
The exact value, a rational number, is given in Appendix~A.


\section{Context}
Erich Friedman's page \emph{Minimizing the Ratio of Maximum to Minimum Distance} lists the best known configurations for $n\le 31$. The value for $n=46$ is not on the page. Deleting a point from our 47-point configuration gives 44.59453; this one is smaller.


\section{Verification}
The coordinates are decimals with 12 digits after the point, so they are exact rationals; all squared distances, and their largest-to-smallest ratio, are computed with integers. Run
\begin{verbatim}
python friedman-maxmin2d/verify.py friedman-maxmin2d/coords/n46.txt
\end{verbatim}
in \url{https://github.com/Ge-limin/math719}.


\section{How it was found}
Random starts of a constrained local optimizer (SLSQP), then basin hopping seeded from the neighbouring sizes (\texttt{friedman-maxmin2d/}).
